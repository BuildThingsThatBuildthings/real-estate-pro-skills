## Voice essence

A friendly person who is good at logistics. She says hello, tells you the useful thing in
plain words, asks if you need anything else, and gets out of the way. Warm, brief,
exclamation-friendly, practical, helpful. The hospitality and events background shows up as
clarity and care (exact times, exact address, exactly how to reach her), never as hype.

## Audience

People looking at homes in and around Pleasant Hill and Walnut Creek, their agents, and the
neighbors in Ridgeview II. They scroll fast and want the price, the bedroom count, what has
already been replaced, and when they can walk through it. They say "HOA", "open house",
"updated", "garage". They do not say "residence" or "appointed".

## Tone attributes

1. **Warm first.** A short human line before or after the facts.
   *"Come say hi Saturday or Sunday, 1 to 4!"*
2. **Brief.** Short sentences, one idea each. Stop when the useful part is said.
   *"Three skylights. One brick fireplace, floor to ceiling. $699,000."*
3. **Practical.** The unglamorous upgrades get named, because they are what a buyer pays
   for later. *"Replacement windows. Newer HVAC, water heater and electrical subpanel. Done."*
4. **Helpful.** End with an offer and one way to take it.
   *"Want a private tour? Call or text me at (925) 899-7926."*
5. **Exclamation-friendly, with restraint.** One or two per caption at most, on the
   greeting or the invitation, never on a fact or a price.

## Mechanical rules

- Sentence length: 3 to 14 words. Fragments are fine for a list of features.
- Paragraph length: 1 to 3 short sentences. White space between ideas.
- Contractions: yes (it's, you'll, here's, I'd).
- Point of view: first person ("I", "my listing", "me") in captions, emails, texts and her
  own recording scripts. Film narration is third person and never speaks as Vanessa or Pam.
- Punctuation: periods and the occasional exclamation mark. Questions are welcome. No em
  dashes or en dashes. No hashtags. No emoji unless she adds them herself.
- Wording of facts follows the listing: "replacement windows", "newer" HVAC, water heater
  and subpanel. Never upgrade "newer" to "brand new".
- Numbers: digits, exact, from facts.json only. $699,000. 1,378 square feet. 1984.
  $598 a month. "More than $120,000" is never rounded up or restated as "over $125K".
- "LMK" and similar abbreviations: fine in texts and DMs, not in captions or email.
- Call to action style: one specific next step per piece. The open house times (Sat Sep 19
  and Sun Sep 20, 1 to 4 pm, Tue Sep 22, 11 am to 1 pm), or "call or text me at
  (925) 899-7926", or the tour link https://164-southwind-dr.netlify.app. Asked the way she
  asked Ryan: a question, then the way to say yes.

## Banned words

- stunning (name the feature and its number)
- boasts (use "has")
- welcome home (open with the claim)
- don't miss (give the time and the address)
- dream home (describe the property)
- nestled (say where it is)
- must-see (state the feature)
- hidden gem (say what is unusual)
- perfect for (describe the room, not a person)
- family-friendly (describe the amenity)
- walking distance (use "near")
- charming (name the material)
- gorgeous (name the material)
- luxurious (name the fixture)
- spacious (give the square footage)
- turnkey (list what was replaced)
- oasis (say "deck" or "pool")
- retreat (say "primary suite")
- entertainer's (describe the layout)
- pride of ownership (list the work)
- won't last (no urgency claims)
- priced to sell (no price claims)
- unlock (shared ban)
- leverage (shared ban)
- seamlessly (shared ban)
- effortlessly (shared ban)

## Per channel

- LinkedIn: the most complete version. Lead with the $120,000 claim, then list where it
  went as evidence. Professional, still plain. Ends on a question or the broker-friendly
  next step. Link goes in the first comment.
- X: the sharpest single idea, under 280 characters including the compliance line. No URL
  in the body. The tour link goes in a reply.
- Instagram: spoken voice. First line is the hook and stands alone under 100 characters.
  Short lines, then the open house times.
- TikTok: shortest spoken version. Hook, two or three concrete nouns, the next step.
- Facebook: warmest and most neighborly. A sentence or two longer, written to be forwarded
  to someone who is looking. Community amenities are named, residents never are.
- YouTube (title and caption): title under 100 characters, address and the concrete claim
  first. Description repeats the facts in full sentences and carries the long-form link.
- Threads: conversational, one thought and a question that invites a reply.
- Google Business: plain, local, factual. Address, price, beds and baths, open house
  times, phone number.

Replacement windows. Newer heating and air, water heater and electrical subpanel. Two
remodeled baths. Composite decking out back.

Come see it Saturday or Sunday, 1 to 4, at 164 Southwind Drive. Questions? Call or text me
at (925) 899-7926!"

This is an original draft shaped by one email, not a quotation from Vanessa.

## Off brand example

"Welcome home to this stunning, light-filled retreat nestled in a sought-after community!
This turnkey gem boasts gorgeous upgrades throughout and is perfect for the discerning
buyer. Don't miss your chance, this one won't last!"

Why it fails: no number, no concrete noun, could describe any house, leans on urgency, and
"perfect for" points at a buyer instead of the property.

## Quality test

Would Vanessa type this to a friend who asked "what's the deal with your new listing?", and
does every sentence carry a number or a thing you could point at in the photos?
